# Booking channel: airline direct vs OTA vs consolidator

Use when the traveler asks "should I book this on Expedia / FlightHub / Justfly / Ovago instead of the airline?"
or sends a fare link and asks about the risk.

## Step 0 — Read the actual seller off the page, not off the traveler's sentence

A traveler often names one site and sends a link that lists four others. Open the link and read the **booking
options** block before answering.

- Google Flights "share" links land on a **booking/options page**, not an airline page. The sellers listed there
  (FlightHub, Justfly, Ovago, AranGrant, etc.) are **third-party OTAs**, and the words "Expedia" and "airline"
  describe neither of them.
- Read the itinerary off the page too: **operating carriers, stop count, elapsed time, bags, and price** all sit
  in one text block. A three-carrier, two-stop, 26h routing is a different product from a single-carrier one, and
  the answer changes with it.
- If the traveler's word and the page disagree, **correct the premise first, then answer the question they meant.**
  Answering the literal question about a seller who isn't on the page wastes the whole turn.

## The decision table

| | Airline direct | OTA (FlightHub, Justfly, Ovago, Expedia) |
|---|---|---|
| Price | usually highest, occasionally matched | lowest, often materially so on multi-carrier itineraries |
| Who owns the booking | the carrier | the agency; the carrier can *see* it but often cannot *change* it |
| Irregular ops (misconnect, cancellation) | carrier rebooks under its own rules | agency call-centre queue; carrier owes you nothing on a ticket it did not sell |
| Changes / refunds | direct, airline's fee only | agency fee **on top of** the airline penalty (commonly $30–50 per change) |
| Seat assignment, upgrades | normal | frequently unavailable until check-in on consolidator fares |
| Bag rules on a multi-carrier ticket | each segment's own rule | "2 free checked bags" is the **most favourable** rule on the itinerary, not necessarily the long-haul leg's |

## The distinction that matters more than OTA-vs-airline

**One ticket vs separate tickets.** On a single ticket with interline carriage, a misconnect is the carriers'
problem. On separate tickets, every misconnect is the traveler's problem — no rebooking, no hotel, no refund.

- The booking page usually does **not** say which you are getting. **Ask the seller to state in writing that it
  is one ticket / one PNR before purchase.** That one sentence is worth more than any price difference, and it
  is the only thing that changes the answer to "what if I miss my connection?".

## The two questions travelers actually ask, answered

**"What if there's an emergency and I need to come home?"** — On an OTA ticket you call the agency, not the
carrier; expect a queue and a fee. The honest fix is **trip-interruption / trip-cancellation insurance**, which
costs far less than a flexible fare and converts the risk into a claim.

**"What if I miss my next flight?"** — One ticket: carriers rebook you. Separate tickets: you buy a new one.
Separately, check the **connection buffer**: a 2-stop, 3-carrier routing with tight ATL/ICN-style transfers is
where a single delay cascades; a longer, simpler routing (hub first, then ground transfer) is often slower
on paper and faster in reality.

## Presentation rules

- Give **pros and cons in the traveler's terms** (price, control, who answers the phone), not in industry terms
  (consolidator, ARC, GDS) unless the traveler uses them first.
- **Name the one thing to confirm before buying** and make it a single sentence they can paste to the seller.
- **Do not tell them a fare is "the best" without searching alternatives** — if you only read their link, say so
  explicitly and offer the comparison rather than implying one.
- **Late arrivals matter**: a 1–2 AM landing at a secondary airport needs the ground transfer arranged in
  advance, and that belongs in the recommendation next to the price.
- **Multi-carrier tickets break baggage promises.** State the free-bag count as "the itinerary's most favourable
  rule" and price the carry-on-only option, because the check-a-bag cost can erase the OTA saving.
