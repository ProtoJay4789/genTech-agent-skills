# The SEC publisher lane — definition, exclusion, and what breaks it

Condensed from the statute and the controlling case; **re-fetch before quoting**, because the exclusion
wording is what lawyers argue about.

## The definition includes publishing, which is why the exclusion exists

**15 U.S.C. §80b-2(a)(11)** — an "investment adviser" is any person who, **for compensation**, engages in the
business of **advising others, either directly or through publications or writings, as to the value of
securities or as to the advisability of investing in, purchasing, or selling securities**, or who for
compensation and as part of a regular business issues or promulgates analyses or reports concerning
securities.

**Read that twice:** "through publications or writings" is *inside* the definition. Publishing does not take
you out of it.

## The exclusion, verbatim

**§80b-2(a)(11)(D)** removes *"the publisher of any bona fide newspaper, news magazine or business or financial
publication of general and regular circulation."*

**Lowe v. SEC, 472 U.S. 181 (1985)** read that exclusion to protect **non-personalised** commentary of
**general and regular circulation**, and held the SEC could not force a newsletter publisher to register.

## What makes a publication stop being "bona fide" — three tripping points

Summaries of *Lowe* and its progeny converge on three: a publication is **not** bona fide if it

1. is a **personal** communication rather than general circulation;
2. contains **false or misleading** information; or
3. is **designed to tout a security in which the publisher has an interest**.

Point 3 governs a show about our own book: it is satisfied by **disclosure** and broken by silence. Point 2 is
why every number carries a date and a source — "misleading" is the prong that strips the exclusion, and
stale-by-a-week is misleading.

## A modern data point, with its caveat

A 2024 S.D.N.Y. decision dismissed a class action alleging a financial-content platform was an unregistered
adviser, applying the publisher exclusion. **Known from law-firm summaries, not from the opinion** — cite it as
"secondary summaries of the decision" unless you have fetched the opinion itself.

## What to keep as evidence

- the classification (publication / recommendation / marketing) in the brief;
- a per-episode **position card** with size and last-trade date;
- **as-of stamps** on every number;
- the archived source for every external claim (a live URL can be edited between record and publish).
