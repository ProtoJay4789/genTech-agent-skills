# Platform policy — the gate that is separate from law

A channel can be legally clean and still unmonetisable. These rules decide the second thing. **Fetch and quote
from the help pages; policies change without notice and aggregator blogs restate them wrong.** Record the
retrieval date in whatever you write.

## YouTube — monetisation policy (support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392)

**Automation is allowed; template output is not.** Verbatim:

> *"If you use automated tools or templates to help create your content, the final product must still
> demonstrate your creative vision and provide educational or entertainment value."*

and, under **Generic or repetitive content**:

> *"…content that looks like it's made with a template, or that may feel repetitive to viewers after watching
> several videos in a row from the same channel."*

**The AI-persona rule is the one that decides revenue for a synthetic presenter** — verbatim:

> *"**AI Personas Related to Sensitive Topics** — This policy refers to channels that use AI-generated personas
> to deliver information on sensitive topics. This includes any content that presents itself as a human expert
> providing advice to viewers on topics such as health, legal issues, **finances**, or politics. … channels
> uploading this content will not be allowed to monetize."*

**Design consequences:**
- Do not build a revenue plan on ad income if the presenter is a synthetic persona on a finance topic. Plan the
  product/API as revenue and treat the show as distribution and proof.
- Name the voice as synthetic on screen and attribute opinions to the human who holds them — an explicit "this
  is an agent voice" defeats the "presents itself as a human expert" element better than any disclaimer.
- Never let the persona *advise*. It reads measurements and verdicts.

## YouTube — advertiser-friendly guidelines (answer/6162278)

Profanity is tiered **by location**, and the title counts:

> *"This content will receive **limited ad earnings** — Moderate profanity (like 'shit') **in the title or
> thumbnail**."*
>
> *"This content will receive **no ad earnings** — Stronger profanity (like f\*ck) **used in thumbnails or
> titles**."*

Policies apply to the video, thumbnail, **title**, description and tags. **A title is a monetisation decision.**

## The "inauthentic content" lineage

The July 2025 change is recorded in the official policy-updates log (answer/10008196): YouTube is *"updating our
guidelines to better identify mass-produced and repetitious content … better reflects what 'inauthentic' content
looks like today."* The **enforcing** text lives on the policy page in the words quoted above, not under an
"inauthentic" heading — quote both, and say which page each came from.

## Google Ads — financial products and services (adspolicy/answer/2464998)

- Financial products include *"products and services related to the management or investment of money and
  cryptocurrencies, **including personalized advice**"*; advertisers must comply with local regulation **in
  every targeted location**.
- **Disclosures "can't be posted as roll-over text or made available through another link or tab"** — visible
  without a click or hover.
- Prohibited outright: *"educational sites or blogs on binary options and equivalent products"*, and ad
  destinations *"that provide signals for the trading of complex speculative financial products"*.

## Google Ads — prediction markets (adspolicy/answer/16757872)

- Ads require Google certification; in the US the advertiser must be a CFTC-designated **DCM** or an
  **NFA-authorised brokerage**.
- *"Informational or educational sites and blogs that provide signals for the trading of Exchange-Listed Event
  Contracts"* are **not supported**.

→ Displaying market-implied probabilities as **data, sourced and dated**, is not the prohibited shape. Framing
them as what to trade is.

## FTC — disclosures (FTC "Disclosures 101"; 16 CFR 255.5)

- Material connections *"must be disclosed clearly and conspicuously"* (255.5(a)).
- *"If making an endorsement in a **video**, the disclosure should be in the video and not just in the
  description uploaded with the video."*
- Disclosures are *"likely to be missed if they appear only on an ABOUT ME or profile page, at the end of posts
  or videos, or anywhere that requires a person to click MORE."*

**This is why a description-only position note is not compliance, and why the statements are spoken.**

## UK — FCA (FCA FG24/1, finalised 2024)

- A financial promotion is an invitation or inducement to engage in investment activity; the rules are
  **technology neutral** and cover social media.
- Promotions must be **fair, clear and not misleading**, with a balanced view of benefits and risks.
- **"Unauthorised persons, such as social media influencers, who promote a regulated financial product or
  service without approval of an appropriate FCA-authorised person may be committing a criminal offence."**

→ A US publisher-style disclaimer does not cure a UK financial promotion. Jurisdiction is a separate analysis,
not a footnote.
