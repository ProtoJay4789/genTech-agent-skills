# Selloff scope, attribution, and what a broken level actually means

Depends on the attribution procedure in SKILL.md (timestamps decide a cause). This file answers the
question that comes BEFORE it: **is this a crypto event at all?**

## 1. Measure the SCOPE before hunting the CAUSE

**"Why did X dump — did we get bad news?" is two questions, and the second one is unanswerable until the
first is settled.** A crypto-specific event (exchange, hack, regulatory action) leaves equities untouched
and gold unbought. A macro de-risking takes the whole risk complex down at once, and crypto is simply the
highest-beta leg of it.

**Measure the same session across the risk complex:**

| asset | ticker | what it separates |
|---|---|---|
| equity index | `SPY` / `QQQ` | broad vs tech-led |
| gold | `GLD` | real-money hedge demand |
| long bonds | `TLT` | flight to safety |
| volatility | `VIXY` | how violent the repricing was |

| crypto | equities | gold / bonds | reading |
|---|---|---|---|
| down | down **more** | bid | **macro de-risking** — crypto is the high-beta leg, not the story |
| down | roughly flat | flat | **crypto-specific** — go look for the catalyst |
| down | down | gold also down | genuine liquidation; the worst case |

**Measured on one session:** BTC −6.32% alongside **QQQ −6.49%**, SPY −4.41%, **GLD +0.81%**, TLT +0.21%,
**VIXY +29.71%**. **The equity index fell further than bitcoin and volatility nearly doubled** — a risk-off
rotation. Answering that with a crypto catalyst would have been confidently wrong.

**State the inference as inference.** The honest sentence is *"no crypto-specific catalyst is visible in the
tape; the pattern is consistent with de-risking into [scheduled event]"*. It is **not** *"the payrolls print
caused it"* — that is an attribution claim and it needs the timestamp-and-volume test in SKILL.md.

**The prior session can contain the tell.** Gold rising while equities print highs and volatility collapses
is **hedging into strength**, and the reversal tends to arrive within a session or two. It is the one signal
visible before the drop, and it is worth naming afterwards.

## 2. A round number is not a level

When asked *"what happens if we break $X"*, **check whether `$X` is a measured level before describing the
level.** Round numbers are self-fulfilling only in the very short term; they are not structure.

- **Name the nearest MEASURED level and the gap to it** — the last confirmed swing low, the window low.
- **If there is nothing between the round number and that level, say so**, and quote the gap as a
  percentage. *"There is no measured support in the 17.9% between here and the window low"* is the answer,
  and it is far more useful than a false floor.
- **Say which it is: a reference or a floor.** The lowest print in a window is a **reference point**, not a
  level anything is defending. Do not let the two blur into one.

## 3. A failed breakout takes its own base with it

**A breakout that fails invalidates the level it was built on, not the high it failed to hold.**

Measured sequence:

```
session A   low 75,601   high 81,131   close 80,428   <- new high, closed near it
session B   open 80,428  high 80,844   low 75,035    <- took out A's low entirely
```

**The level that mattered was 75,601** — the floor the breakout was built on — and it was **already gone** by
the time anyone asked what happens next. The high was never the question.

- **Separate "the price fell" from "the sequence broke".** A new high fully retraced is a *failed breakout*.
  A prior low undercut is a *structure break*. Different consequences, routinely reported as one event.
- **List every swing low above spot when a break happens.** If all of them are broken, the position is
  **in an air pocket** and the honest answer names the gap rather than the next round number.

## 4. Reporting shape that worked

1. What happened, with the **hour and the volume** of the break (a move with no volume is drift).
2. The cross-asset table, so the reader can see the scope for themselves.
3. The classification: macro de-risking vs crypto-specific, stated as a reading of the tape.
4. The levels: which are broken, which is the last one, and the size of the gap beneath it.
5. What it changes in the book — frequently **nothing**, and say that plainly.
6. The one number that would settle the next question, so it becomes watched rather than re-argued.
