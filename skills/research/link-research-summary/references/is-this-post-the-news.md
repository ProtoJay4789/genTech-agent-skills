# Is this post the NEWS, or a follow-up to it?

**The failure this prevents:** a major account posts a high-engagement statement, and the summary reports it as
the event. The event often happened **days earlier**; the post is an apology, a denial, a clarification, or a
public bid to get a job back. Reporting the post as the news is an error the user catches with one search.

**Measured shape:** a founder-CEO's post read as *"the CEO just resigned"* — he had resigned five days before, and
the post was him asking the board to reinstate him, published hours before earnings. Same tweet, two entirely
different stories.

---

## Procedure — run it BEFORE writing the summary

1. **Read `created_at` and subtract it from today.** fxtwitter returns it; a post from today about a crisis may
   sit days behind the crisis.
2. **Search the EVENT, not the post.** `<person> resigns <company>` · `<company> names CEO` · `<company> Q3
   results`. If coverage exists from earlier, the post is a sequel and **the timeline is the story**.
3. **Confirm with two named outlets before calling it fact.** One account, however prominent, is one source.
   Compare what the post *claims* with what the outlets *report*; the gap is usually the real angle (a public
   campaign running alongside an active investigation, a statement timed to an earnings call).
4. **Treat likes and views as reach, not truth.** Millions of impressions prove attention.
5. **Separate in the answer: what happened · when · what this post adds.** "He resigned five days ago; this is
   him asking to come back" is a better summary than either half alone.

## Then do the relevance pass that makes it worth reading

A market or industry event is only useful to the user once it is tied to **their** exposure — and vague
atmosphere ("uncertainty in AI") is not relevance. Name the concrete lines:

- **What we depend on it for** — a supplier, a rail, a price, a counterparty.
- **What expires or renews inside the affected window** — a key, a contract, a quota.
- **What would make it our problem** — and what would prove it is not.

**State plainly what you are NOT claiming** ("a tweet is not a price"). Users trust a bounded claim; an
unbounded one invites them to check, and they will find the seam.

## Pitfall: the sensational claim is not permission to skip the brain check

Before analysing, search the vault and session history for the subject — a prior note may already hold the
regulatory history, the position, or the reason we care. The event may be new; **our exposure to it rarely is.**
